# 04 - Basis for the declared-colour marker and the butter case (read 2026-11-20)

Scope: documentation only. No rule was added or changed by this note. The declared-colour marker stays as it was,
PROVISIONALLY. Foodscan gives a label-based NOVA classification. It is not a finding about the safety of any additive
(annatto included) or about the health effects of any product.

   Baraldi LG, Jaime PC. "Ultra-processed foods: what they are and how to identify them." Public Health Nutrition 2019;
   22(5):936-941. doi:10.1017/S1368980018003762, PMID 30744710 (PMC10260459 is its PubMed Central copy).
   READ: the abstract only, via the Europe PMC API (https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=PMCID:PMC10260459&resultType=core).
   NOT READ: the full text (pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov answered with a browser-check challenge, which was not bypassed).
   Label: this is the NOVA group's 2019 identification paper. It is NOT the 2010 NOVA paper and should not be cited as such.
   What I could read is the abstract only; statements below that go beyond the abstract rest on source 2, not on this paper.
   Abstract wording used: ultra-processed products can be identified by "at least one item characteristic of the NOVA
   ultra-processed food group, ... either food substances never or rarely used in kitchens (such as high-fructose corn
   syrup, hydrogenated or interesterified oils, and hydrolysed proteins), or classes of additives designed to make the
   final product palatable or more appealing (such as flavours, flavour enhancers, colours, emulsifiers, emulsifying
   salts, sweeteners, thickeners, and anti-foaming, bulking, carbonating, foaming, gelling and glazing agents)".
2. NUPENS/USP, "Learn how to classify foods" (page dated 5 March 2018),
   https://www.fsp.usp.br/nupens/en/learn-how-to-classify-foods/ (redirects to nupens.fsp.usp.br). READ in full.
   - Group 2 examples: "butter and lard obtained from milk and pork". "Products consisting of two group 2 items, such as
     salted butter ... remain in this group. Group 2 items may contain additives used to preserve the product's original
     properties" (examples given: antioxidants in oils, anti-humectants in salt, preservatives in vinegar).
   - Group 4: "When products made solely of group 1 or group 3 foods also contain cosmetic or sensory intensifying
     additives, such as plain yoghurt with added artificial sweeteners, and breads with added emulsifiers, they are
     classified here in group 4." Listed classes restricted to group 4 include "colourings" (and also thickeners,
     stabilisers, anti-caking agents, which foodscan treats as "unlisted"/uncertain: a known difference, not changed here).
3. FAO/WHO GSFA Online, additives by technological function (colour):

## What the sources do and do not establish
- Plain or salted butter (milk fat, with or without salt) is a Group 2 example (source 2).
- A product that DECLARES an added colour is, under the NOVA wording above, classified in Group 4 when the colour is a
  cosmetic additive in the declared-additive sense (sources 1 and 2). Neither source states that a group 2 product with an
  added colour stays in group 2, and neither states a natural-versus-synthetic exemption. The sources say "colours" and
  "colourings" without qualification; they do not discuss annatto specifically. (Annatto's own status as a natural-origin
  colour is not covered by either source: unverified here.)
- Therefore: "butter" and "salted butter" differ from "butter with a natural colour (annatto)" only by that declared colour.
  The existing behaviour (Group 4 on a declared colour) is kept, provisionally, as the closest reading of the sources. It is
  not an unconditional "any colour word = Group 4" rule; see the limits below.

## Limits that stay in force (tests: tests/test_colour_marker_scope.py)
- The marker needs a DECLARED additive colour (class word "colour/color", an INS colour code, or a "Contains ... colour"
  declaration). A naturally coloured whole ingredient (turmeric, beetroot powder, saffron, annatto seed listed as an
  ingredient with no colour declaration) is never read as an additive; unrecognised ones appear under "Not assessed".
- If the identity, the declared function or the photo reading is uncertain, the existing uncertainty paths apply
  (truncation, heading and completeness signal). Group 4 on a marker survives only under the documented Option A rule,
  and the result carries the warning.
- Open for the project owner, not for this pass: whether thickeners/stabilisers/anti-caking agents, which source 2 also
  lists as group-4 classes, should become markers. Not done; no new rules are approved.

## Vocabulary citations and the milk-plus-culture rule (2026-11-20)
- NUPENS-USP page = NOVA METHOD source only (what group a food falls in). It does not define besan, toned milk or dahi.
- FSSAI, Food Safety and Standards regulations, Chapter 2 Food Product Standards, 2.1 Dairy products and analogues:
  https://www.fssai.gov.in/upload/uploadfiles/files/2_%20Chapter%202_1%20(Dairy%20products%20and%20analogues).pdf
  (fetched 2026-11-20; 105 pages; PDF created 2023-05-23; SHA-256 94a52a70248b15e346a53a4b532259552868296e00f7336517258e9d685944bc;
  amendments noted in the text as in force 1 Aug 2023). Names, with section: "Toned Milk" 3.0% fat / 8.5% SNF and "Double Toned Milk"
  1.5% fat / 9.0% SNF (1.3% from 1 Aug 2023) in 2.1.2 item 1(c) and its table; "Dahi" characterised by lactic acid bacteria in 2.1.13
  item 1(a), where "Dahi (Curd)" is printed and an amendment omits the word (Curd). It names starter cultures of harmless
  microorganisms (2.1.13) but none of the exact phrases in the culture rows, and it does not mention besan.
- Rule for every vocabulary row: the cited source names that exact term, or the row stays PROVISIONAL with the missing source stated.
  besan, gram flour, chickpea flour: PROVISIONAL, source missing. curd -> dahi: PROVISIONAL. Culture rows: PROVISIONAL, exact term not in a source.
- Milk-plus-culture rule (classify.py): when the WHOLE list is recognised and consists only of milk (milk, toned milk, double toned milk)
  and starter culture(s), with nothing else, the result is Group 1 citing the NUPENS-USP page (plain yoghurt with no added sugar or
  artificial sweeteners; fermentation of milk to make yoghurt). Any other item, marker or unrecognised word keeps the existing behaviour.
  It is not keyed off the words yoghurt, dahi or curd.

## Fix round (2026-11-20): vocabulary and role phrases
- buffalo milk: Identity from FSSAI 2.1.2 item 1(a) and the composition table in item 2(b) (Buffalo Milk, minimum 5.0% milk fat, 9.0% SNF). NOVA step PROVISIONAL like the other milks. It is a milk term of the milk-plus-culture rule.
- probiotic culture: NOT mapped to the culture kind. FSSAI 2.1.13 item 2(b) lists "(i) starter cultures of harmless microorganisms" and, separately, "(viii) Prebiotics and Probiotics" as permitted ingredients of fermented milks, so a probiotic addition is not established as a fermentation starter. Lists that carry it stay uncertain.
- emulsifying agent / stabilizing agent (and the combined phrase): role phrases for the emulsifier and stabiliser classes. PROVISIONAL, no source names the phrase. They change no group by themselves: an emulsifier is a Group 4 class (NUPENS-USP), a stabiliser stays an unlisted class here; codes decide only where ins_map.csv already carries a class.

## Addendum (2026-11-20): dairy additions considered and NOT added (owner direction)
- Rule applied: an FSSAI definition supports a product's identity only, never its NOVA group. A confident group needs an explicit NOVA-method basis (NUPENS-USP page: milk, pasteurised or powdered, is group 1); otherwise the result abstains, which is an acceptable answer.
- Milk with added milk solids ("Milk, Milk solids"): FSSAI 2.1.2 item 1(c) lets toned, double toned, standardised, full cream and skimmed milk be made "with or without dried or concentrated milks or milk fat" (identity only). The NOVA page names milk, pasteurised or powdered, as group 1 but does not say a blend of the two stays group 1, nor that "milk solids" equals powdered milk. No rule added: such lists abstain ("combination does not match a group 1, 2 or 3 pattern").
- cow milk: FSSAI 2.1.2 item 1(a) names "cow milk" (identity). The NOVA page names "milk" only. No row added: "Pasteurized Cow Milk" abstains as outside the vocabulary.
- Vitamins: FSSAI 2.1.1 item 4 allows milk to be fortified with "vitamins, minerals, etc." (permission only; names no vitamin for milk). No read NOVA page says fortified milk stays in group 1. Lists with vitamins keep abstaining.
- Existing rows carry the same kind of inference (toned milk, double toned milk, buffalo milk treated as milk; milk solids treated as powdered milk; all PROVISIONAL): flagged to the owner for a decision, not changed here.

## Owner decision (2026-11-20): existing PROVISIONAL dairy rows stay
- toned milk, double toned milk, buffalo milk are read as milk: two-part basis = identity (FSSAI 2.1.2 item 1(a)/(c) and the composition table) plus method (NUPENS-USP page: milk, pasteurised or powdered, is group 1). milk solids is read as powdered milk: identity (FSSAI 2.1.13 item 2(a)(ii) lists dried and concentrated milk) plus the same NUPENS-USP example. All four stay visibly PROVISIONAL (the word appears in the source text shown with each driver).
- Not covered by that two-part basis, so no rule: milk with added milk solids, cow milk, fortified milk. No new dairy cases.

## Open question for a later sourcing pass (owner decision 2026-11-20): humectant and firming agent
markers.csv counts "humectant" and "firming" as strong cosmetic_additive markers on the basis of research/02 (cosmetic processing aids, from Open Food Facts material). The FAO 2019 report (research/02 addendum) does not name humectants or firming agents anywhere in its 44 pages.
Absence from that text is not proof that they are not markers (the same discipline as for acidity regulators), so they are neither removed nor re-sourced this round. To close: find a primary NOVA text that names or excludes them (for example the Public Health Nutrition 2019 identification paper in full, or the NUPENS-USP page), then confirm or remove the rows.
