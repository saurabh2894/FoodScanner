Knowledge base for the foodscan classifier. Compiled 20 Nov 2026 from public
sources listed at the end. Where a rule in the code comes from this file, the

## The four NOVA groups

NOVA classifies foods by the extent and purpose of industrial processing, not
by nutrients. It was developed by researchers at the University of Sao Paulo,
Brazil, and is the most widely used public processing classification.

- **Group 1 - Unprocessed or minimally processed foods.** Whole foods with no
  or minimal alteration: cleaning, drying, grinding, freezing, pasteurising.
  Examples: atta, rice, milk, eggs, fruit, plain dals.
- **Group 2 - Processed culinary ingredients.** Substances extracted from group
  1 foods or from nature, used in cooking: oils, ghee, sugar, salt.
- **Group 3 - Processed foods.** Group 1 foods combined with group 2 substances
  using recognisable preservation or cooking methods: canned vegetables in
  brine, cheese, simple unpackaged breads.
- **Group 4 - Ultra-processed foods.** Industrial formulations made mostly or
  entirely from substances extracted from foods or synthesised in factories,
  with little or no intact group 1 food.

## What an ingredient list can and cannot tell you

- The ingredient list is strong evidence for Group 4: the marker classes below
  rarely appear in groups 1 to 3.
- It is weaker evidence at the group 1/2/3 boundary. Two products with the same
  ingredients can be processed differently, and the label does not always say
  how an ingredient was made (for example whether a bread is bakery-fresh or an
  industrial formulation).
- There is no ingredient-count rule in NOVA. A short ingredient list can still
  be ultra-processed, and a long list of recognisable foods can be group 3.
- When the list is missing, incomplete, or the processing context is decisive
  and unknown, the correct output is "not enough information", not a guess.

## Marker classes associated with Group 4

These classes are documented in the NOVA literature and in Open Food Facts'
published ingredients analysis as indicators of ultra-processing. Treat them as
strong indicators, not absolute proof, and validate the final mapping against
the two sources below while building:

1. **Substances derived from foods that rarely appear in home kitchens:**
   hydrogenated or interesterified vegetable oil, high fructose corn syrup /
   glucose-fructose syrup, invert sugar, maltodextrin, dextrose, modified
   starches, protein isolates (soy protein isolate, whey protein isolate).
2. **Cosmetic additive classes** (they imitate or intensify sensory
   qualities): colourings, flavourings and flavour enhancers, non-sugar
   sweeteners, emulsifiers, and cosmetic processing aids such as anti-foaming,
   bulking, carbonating, foaming, gelling, glazing, firming and humectant
   agents.

Important qualifications:

- A single ordinary additive does not automatically make a product group 4.
  Pectin (INS 440) in a jam or raising agents in a plain bread are consistent
  with group 3. When the only marker is a weak or ambiguous one, prefer
  "uncertain" over a forced call.
- Legality is not the question. An additive can be fully permitted in India
  and still mark the product as ultra-processed. Keep those claims separate.

## Indian label specifics

- Indian packs declare additives by functional class and INS number, for
  example "Emulsifier (INS 471)". INS numbers follow the same international
  numbering as European E numbers: INS 171 is E171. The parser must handle both
  spellings.
- Ingredients appear in descending order of weight under FSSAI labelling rules.
- Labels may mix English and Hindi, and use local names (for example "edible
  vegetable oil (palmolein)").

## Synthetic examples for tests

These are invented examples for unit tests, not validated classifications of
real products.

- "Ingredients: Whole wheat flour." -> Group 1.
- "Ingredients: Refined wheat flour, sugar, edible vegetable oil (palmolein),
  invert syrup, raising agents (INS 500(ii), INS 503(ii)), emulsifier
  (INS 471), salt, added flavour (vanilla)." -> Group 4 (derived substances
  plus flavouring and emulsifier markers).
- "Ingredients: Cucumber, water, salt, vinegar, dill." -> Group 3.
- Partial text: "Ingredients: Sugar, refined wheat flo" -> Not enough
  information (truncated list).
