06 - Declared role of additive codes: the NOVA method text that was read (2026-11-20)

PURPOSE. Each additive-code row can show the class the scanned label itself declares for the entry the code sits in (src/foodscan/roles.py,
resources/declared_roles.csv). The wording about what that role means for Group 4 is taken only from the NOVA method text below. Display only:
no classification depends on it (tests/test_declared_roles.py proves identical group, reason and drivers with the feature switched off).

PRIMARY SOURCE READ IN FULL (all 44 pages as text, searched for every class word):
  Monteiro CA, Cannon G, Lawrence M, Costa Louzada ML, Pereira Machado P. 2019. Ultra-processed foods, diet quality, and health using the NOVA
  classification system. Rome, FAO.   https://www.fao.org/3/ca5644en/ca5644en.pdf   (PDF fetched 2026-11-20, SHA-256 c8024c194a8294a0...)
  The same authors' Public Health Nutrition 2019 paper ("Ultra-processed foods: what they are and how to identify them") could not be read in full:
  PMC answered with a reCAPTCHA page and cambridge.org / europepmc.org are not reachable from this environment; only its abstract was read earlier.

WHAT THE TEXT SAYS (report page numbers as printed in the footer):
  p. 9   "Classes of additives used only in the manufacture of ultra-processed foods, are flavours, flavour enhancers, colours, emulsifiers, emulsifying
         salts, artificial sweeteners, thickeners, and foaming, anti-foaming, bulking, carbonating, gelling and glazing agents."
  p. 10  "Classes of additive exclusively used in ultra-processed foods ... include flavours, flavour enhancers, colours, emulsifiers, emulsifying salts,
         artificial sweeteners, thickeners, and anti-foaming, bulking, carbonating, foaming, gelling and glazing agents. Any example of these classes of
         additive, as shown on ingredients lists also identifies a product as ultra-processed."
  p. 11  Table 1, Group 3: "They may contain additives that prolong product duration, protect original properties, or prevent proliferation of
         microorganisms." Table 1, Group 4 also lists "additives that prolong product duration, protect original properties or prevent proliferation of
         microorganisms" among what ultra-processed formulations contain.
  p. 12  Group 3 examples: "canned fish (with or without added preservatives); fruit in syrup (with or without added anti-oxidants)".
  Not named anywhere in the 44 pages (searched: acidity, regulator, stabili*, caking, flour treatment, raising, humectant, firming, salt substitute): acidity
  regulators, stabilisers, anticaking agents, flour treatment agents, raising agents, humectants, firming agents, salt substitutes.

WORDING RULES DERIVED (declared_roles.csv, column nova_status)
  group4_listed  (the class is on the pp. 9-10 list): "Declared as X: the NOVA source lists X among the additive classes of ultra-processed foods", then what
                 this tool does with the class word.
  group3_allowed (preservative, antioxidant): "Declared as X: no Group 4 marker detected for this role per [source]", with the qualification that the source
                 mentions such additives in both Group 3 and the Group 4 definition, so the role does not by itself separate the groups. Never "never a marker".
  not_named      (acidity regulator, stabiliser, anticaking agent, flour treatment agent, raising agent, humectant, firming agent, salt substitute): "this class is
                 not named in the NOVA source read"; no "no marker" claim is made.
  A code whose label declares no class keeps the bare wording. A class is never read from a code.

FINDINGS THAT AFFECT CLASSIFICATION (flagged to the owner, NOT changed in this round)
  1. thickeners are on the source's list of Group 4 additive classes (pp. 9-10) but markers.csv holds "thickener" as unlisted ("NOT in research/02"), so a
     label declaring "Thickeners (INS 508)" stays uncertain. The row now says exactly this. Whether to count the class is the owner's decision.
  2. emulsifying salts are on the same list; markers.csv has no row for them.
  3. humectant and firming agent are counted as strong markers here (research/02, from Open Food Facts material) but are not named in the FAO 2019 text.
